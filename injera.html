Injera

A spongy, slightly sour flatbread made from teff, used as plate and spoon.

⏱ 3 days (mostly fermenting) 🍽 Serves 4

About this dish

Injera is the heart of Ethiopian and Eritrean meals. Made from teff, a tiny ancient grain, the batter is fermented for a few days, then cooked on a large round griddle called a mitad. Stews (wat) are served on top, and the bread itself is torn and used to scoop up each bite, which is why sharing injera is such a social act.

Ingredients

Servings: 4
  • 2 cups teff flour
  • 3 cups water, plus more to thin
  • 0.5 tsp salt
  • A spoonful of previous batter (ersho) as starter, optional

Cooking instructions

Step-by-step method
  1. Whisk the teff flour with the water until smooth. Cover loosely with a cloth.
  2. Ferment at room temperature for 2-3 days. It is ready when bubbly and pleasantly sour, with a layer of water on top.
  3. Pour off the water, add the salt, and stir. Thin with water until the batter is like thin pancake batter.
  4. Heat a non-stick pan over medium heat. Pour the batter in a spiral from the outside to the centre.
  5. Cover and cook 2-3 minutes. Do not flip: injera is cooked on one side only, and the surface should be covered in small holes.
  6. Cool on a clean towel in a single layer, then serve with your favourite stew.
Chef's tips

Taste and adjust the seasoning at the end. Use the servings buttons above to scale the ingredients, and keep the heat low whenever a recipe says to simmer.
